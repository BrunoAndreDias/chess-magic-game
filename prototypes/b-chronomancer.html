Chronomancer — Time is Mana

What this prototype tests: your chess clock is also your magic. Every spell is paid in seconds taken straight from your own thinking time. Is it interesting to trade “time to think” for “power to act” — or does one side just hoard the clock? Watch the Thinking · Magic · Siphoned tally under each clock.

How to play
  • 6×6 chess. Rook, Knight, Queen, King move as usual. Pawns step 1 forward (no double step), capture diagonally, and become a Queen on the last row. No castling, no check: capturing the King wins.
  • Each turn: optionally cast one spell, then make one move.
  • A spell's cost is removed from your own clock the moment you cast it. You can't cast if it would leave you under 5 seconds.
  • Haste (60 s): two moves this turn, with two different pieces. The second move may not capture the King.
  • Freeze (25 s): an enemy piece (not the King) can't move on their next turn.
  • Rewind (45 s): undo the opponent's whole last turn. Their clock is not refunded. You can't rewind a turn that itself used Rewind.
  • Blink (35 s): teleport one of your pieces (not the King) to an empty square up to 2 away, then move as normal.
  • Siphon (30 s): the opponent's clock runs at double speed during their next turn.
  • You can't cast the same spell two turns in a row.
  • After every turn you gain the increment. Run out of time and you lose. 150 turns total = draw.
