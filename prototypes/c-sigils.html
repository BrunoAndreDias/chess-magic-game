Sigils: Geometry is Magic Prototype C of 3

What this prototype tests: is it fun when spells aren't picked from a hand or a menu, but emerge from the shapes your pieces make on the board? There are no cards and no mana, just two sigils. When a move completes a LINE or a SQUARE of your Adepts, the sigil fires at once. Things to notice: do you start reading the board as geometry (open files and diagonals, half-built squares)? Does the clock make you miss or rush a sigil? Does it feel like its own game, or chess with extra steps?

How to play

Pieces. Each side has 6 Adepts (round) and 1 Oracle (diamond). Adepts step 1 square in any of 8 directions. The Oracle steps 1 square orthogonally. Move onto an enemy piece to capture it.

You never cast spells. Shapes do. After you move an Adept, every sigil that includes that Adept and is made only of your Adepts fires automatically. The Oracle never counts. Formations that are already standing stay dormant until you move a piece into or within them.

LINE: Beam. 3 or more Adepts in a straight row (orthogonal or diagonal). A beam fires out of both ends and destroys the first enemy it meets. Your own pieces block it.
Diagonals count too. A beam that reaches the enemy Oracle wins the game.
SQUARE: Bloom. A 2×2 block of your Adepts summons a new Adept on your home row, on the free square closest to the centre (max 8 Adepts).

Winning. Destroy the enemy Oracle (by capture or beam), wipe out every enemy Adept, or let the enemy's clock run out. A player with no legal moves loses. After 150 moves in total the game is a draw.

Tip: select a piece, then hover a highlighted square. The board shows the sigils that move would form.
